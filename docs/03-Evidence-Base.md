## 3. Per-transformation evidence

### Font size — **strong**

- Systematic review of font size and older adults; recommendations of 14 px for search, 17–20 px for
  sustained reading, 18 pt on tablets (ages 62–84), 22 pt optimal (65+) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9376262
- **Ceiling effect:** reading speed declines above roughly 1° of visual angle. Same source.
- 18 pt significantly faster for dyslexic and control readers, n=92, eye tracking — Rello & Baeza-Yates — https://repositori.upf.edu/bitstreams/275f50cd-7524-479a-a09f-1edc22830b30/download

### Text spacing — **strong**

- +2.5 pt inter-letter spacing improved reading speed and **halved errors**, n=94 dyslexic children, no training — Zorzi et al., PNAS 2012 — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22665803/
- WCAG 1.4.12 values derived from McLeish 2007 (benefit rises to 0.25 em, plateau at 0.20), Chung 2012, Rello 2017, Zorzi 2012; ~480 scripts tested without adverse effects — https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html
- The apparent "dyslexia font" benefit in Marinus et al. 2016 was attributable to **spacing, not glyph design** — https://www.shanahanonliteracy.com/blog/what-about-special-fonts-for-kids-with-dyslexia-or-other-reading-problems

### Contrast and polarity — **strong**

- Positive polarity (dark on light) improved acuity and proofreading for both younger and older adults, n=169 — Piepenbrock, Mayr & Buchner, Ergonomics 2013 — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00140139.2013.790485
- **The positive-polarity advantage grows as font size shrinks** — Piepenbrock 2014 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0018720813515509
- **Exception:** readers with cloudy ocular media (cataract, corneal scatter) read 10–15% _faster_ in negative polarity — Legge et al. 1985 — https://legge.psych.umn.edu/sites/legge.psych.umn.edu/files/2020-08/Psychophysics%20of%20Reading%20II%20Low%20Vision%20Legge%20Rubin%20Pelli%20Schleske%201985.pdf
- Do not default to dark mode; let users switch — NN/g — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/
- Colored tint overlays: no reliable reading benefit — Henderson et al. 2013 — https://nasenjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1471-3802.2012.01237.x

### Target size — **strong**

- Touch positions cluster within ~8 mm regardless of target size; small targets are the main tap-error source, 20 older participants — Kobayashi et al., INTERACT 2011 — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221054180_Elderly_User_Evaluation_of_Mobile_Touchscreen_Interactions
- Older adults with normal finger movement best at 16.5 mm; **low flexibility needs ≥19.05 mm** — Jin, Plocher & Kiff 2007 — https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-73279-2_104
- Older adults' movement time 35% lower on touchscreen than mouse, versus 16% for younger — Findlater et al., CHI 2013 — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2470654.2470703
- Target expansion reduces homing-in time for older users — Hwang, Hollinworth & Williams, TACCESS 2013 — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2514848

### Click stabilization — **moderate**

- Freezing the cursor on button-down significantly reduced attempts per selection for the highest-slip users, 11 motor-impaired participants — Trewin, Keates & Moffatt, "Steady Clicks" 2006 — https://research.ibm.com/publications/developing-steady-clicks-a-method-of-cursor-assistance-for-people-with-motor-impairments
- Freeze times tested at 0/200/400/600/800 ms with 24 older adults: **200–400 ms optimal, 800 ms harms drag and drop** — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687017301473
- 28.1% of clicks by motor-impaired users contain movement during the click — Trewin & Pain
- **No direct evidence** that cursor enlargement alone improves web task performance. **No direct evidence** for dwell-click or sticky targets on the web.

### Motion and distraction — **strong**

- Distraction during digital reading reduces comprehension, Hedges' g = −0.64 [−0.89, −0.40] across 32 studies and 124 experiments; larger for adult learners (g ≈ −0.72) — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1671214/full
- ~1% of visitors click a carousel; 89% of those interact only with slide 1 — Notre Dame data via https://vwo.com/blog/image-slider-alternatives/
- Auto-forwarding carousels annoy users and reduce content visibility — NN/g — https://www.nngroup.com/articles/auto-forwarding/
- Cycling content removes it before slower readers finish — WebAIM — https://webaim.org/techniques/carousels/
- **No direct randomized trial** of motion removal on ADHD task performance; formative work only — https://arxiv.org/html/2507.13309

### Reading width — **moderate**

- ~55 characters per line supported effective reading at normal and fast speeds with highest comprehension; 80+ caused loss of place — Dyson & Haselgrove 2001 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581901904586
- Removing eye-movement demands improved comprehension ~13% for ADHD adults versus controls — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41277242/
- Very short lines (30–50 characters) slow reading — https://www.uxpin.com/studio/blog/optimal-line-length-for-readability/

### Reducing density and hiding distractions — **strong for distraction, moderate for older adults**

- Flattened low-complexity structures: mean completion 12.1 s and 0.20 errors, versus 35.1 s and 1.36 errors for deep complex ones; complexity lowered positive affect (β = −0.233). 285 survey plus 25 experimental participants aged 60+ — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13284980/
- **Counter:** older adults are more captured by salient elements but worse at goal-directed search, so hiding the wrong element is costly — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7745017/

### Forms — **moderate to strong**

- Forms improved against published guidelines had faster completion, fewer submission attempts, fewer fixations, higher satisfaction; **error placement adjacent to the field mattered most**. n=65, eye tracking — Seckler et al., CHI 2014 — https://research.google/pubs/designing-usable-web-forms-empirical-evaluation-of-web-form-improvement-guidelines/
- **Counter:** validation firing on field exit produced significantly _more_ errors than post-submit messages — Bargas-Avila et al. 2007 — https://www.mmi-basel.ch/en/projects/09-usability-of-web-forms.html
- Inline validation practitioner test: +22% success, −22% errors, −42% time — https://alistapart.com/article/inline-validation-in-web-forms/
- One-thing-per-page: **qualitative evidence only** — GOV.UK — https://designnotes.blog.gov.uk/2015/07/03/one-thing-per-page/

### Plain-language simplification — **moderate, with a sharp caveat**

- Randomized trial, n=4,563 across 31 texts: model simplification raised multiple-choice comprehension +3.9 pp overall (+14.6 on medical text), sustained ~4 pp without the text present, and lowered perceived difficulty by 0.33 on a 5-point scale — Google 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01980
- **Risk:** simplification systems showed insertion errors in 49% of samples, substitution in 49%, deletion in 7% — Devaraj et al., ACL 2022 — https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.506/
- A 2026 systematic review found insufficient evidence that Easy Read improves comprehension for intellectual disability — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12893875/
- **No direct evidence** for model-based rewriting with older adults.

### Reading aids

- Text to speech improves comprehension for readers with reading disabilities, **d = 0.35 [0.14, 0.56]**, 22 studies, n=2,942 — Wood et al. 2018 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022219416688170 — **strong**
- All four digital reading ruler designs significantly increased reading speed with no comprehension cost, 91 dyslexic and 86 non-dyslexic readers; no single preferred style — CHI 2023 — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581367 — **moderate**
- **Prefix-bolding reading: negative.** n>2,000 readers, 2.6 wpm _slower_, no comprehension difference — https://blog.readwise.io/bionic-reading-results/ ; replication at n=350 found no difference — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380485238_No_Bionic_Reading_does_not_work

### Fonts — **negative for dyslexia-specific fonts**

- OpenDyslexic gave no improvement in reading rate or accuracy versus Arial or Times, and none preferred it — Wery & Diliberto 2017 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5629233/
- Dyslexie font gave no benefit to children with or without dyslexia — Kuster et al. 2018 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5934461/
- Helvetica, Courier, Arial, Verdana improved readability for dyslexic readers; **italics impaired it**; OpenDyslexic did not stand out. n=48, eye tracking — Rello & Baeza-Yates — https://dyslexiahelp.umich.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/good_fonts_for_dyslexia_study.pdf
- Reading speed differs 35% between a person's fastest and slowest font with no comprehension loss, and **stated preference does not predict the fastest font** — Wallace et al., TOCHI 2022 — https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3502222

### Navigation — **moderate in lab, weak for automatic flattening**

- Shallow (≤2 level) text-labeled navigation cut time ~3× and errors ~7× versus deep icon-only; **sense of control outweighed speed** — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13284980/
- **Counter:** mega menus exposing lower levels _reduce_ depth-related disorientation versus tiled menus, so collapsing one can add depth — https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-92037-5_27
- **Counter:** navigation aids did not improve performance for either age group in one experiment — https://journal.gerontechnology.org/archives/736-738-1-PB.pdf

### Primary action emphasis — **weak, indirect**

- Salience-driven attention is stronger in older adults while goal-directed first fixations are impaired — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7745017/
- Increased information saliency improves value-based decisions in both age groups — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0197458020304085
- **No direct controlled evidence** that call-to-action emphasis improves older adults' web task success.

### Focus indicators — **consensus only**

- Parameters are consensus-driven; **no controlled study found** quantifying task effect of focus ring size or contrast — https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-appearance.html

### Location cues — **moderate, mixed**

- Search was more efficient with breadcrumbs and a dynamic side tree for both age groups — https://journal.gerontechnology.org/archives/736-738-1-PB.pdf
- Age differences in search time were eliminated when working memory and processing speed were controlled, implying external memory aids help — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/215520605_Design_Effective_Navigation_Tools_for_Older_Web_Users
- **No direct evidence** for progress indicators with these personas.

## 4. Adaptive interfaces — why "offer, never impose"

| Finding                                                                                                                                                                     | Source                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Static menus significantly faster than adaptive; **users preferred adaptable (user-controlled)**. n=27                                                                      | Findlater & McGrenere, CHI 2004 — https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/tr/2004/findlater04menus/         |
| **Accuracy of adaptation affected performance more than predictability; poor accuracy is worse than no adaptation**                                                         | Gajos et al., CHI 2008 — https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2008/gajos08predictability.shtml |
| Full adaptivity helped older users in routine situations but **sharply increased workload in unfamiliar ones**                                                              | Lavie & Meyer 2010 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581910000145          |
| Inactivity-timer help perceived as a "gentle nudge," more competent and less intrusive than help you must request; **77% wanted AI framed as a tool, not a teammate**. n=66 | IUI 2026 — https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01481v1                                                        |
| Users want **visual suggestions backed by interaction data, not silent changes**                                                                                            | Alves et al., CHI 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19196                                             |
| Designers disagree on personalization choices (κ = 0.25), so personalization must be per-person, not rubric-based                                                           | Peng, Das, Bigham & Wu 2026 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09876                                        |
