## 3. A sample that answers the motor question

### Proposed eligibility

1. Age 50 or older.
2. Uses a smartphone or tablet for web tasks at least weekly.
3. Reports repeated difficulty accurately tapping small or closely spaced controls, or an existing workaround for that difficulty.
4. Can voluntarily agree to the described study and communicate their experience using the study's supported accommodations.
5. Has a device compatible with the specific mobile study website or prototype, or can use a provided compatible device after practice.

Ask about interaction experience instead of requiring proof of arthritis, tremor, stroke, or another diagnosis. W3C describes varied physical barriers and input strategies; this supports testing the person's actual interaction needs. [Physical abilities and barriers](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/abilities-barriers/physical/)

### Suggested screening questions

- Are you 50 or older? Which age band: 50-64, 65-74, or 75+?
- Do you use a smartphone, tablet, or both? What device and browser?
- How often do you tap the wrong button or link because controls are small or close together: never, rarely, sometimes, often?
- Does hand steadiness, stiffness, coordination, discomfort, or fatigue affect tapping? “Prefer not to say” is available.
- When tapping is difficult, what do you usually do: zoom, use a different finger, use a stylus, use accessibility settings, ask for help, or something else?
- Is the difficulty mainly seeing the control, understanding what to do, or physically selecting it? More than one answer is allowed.
- What accommodation or break would help you participate comfortably?

Before enrolling, set a consistent functional rule with Dr. Rani, such as “sometimes/often” on the small-control question plus a relevant hand-control difficulty or workaround. **Do not select participants using an unusually poor score on the same task that will become the main outcome.** That can exaggerate apparent improvement on retest. Use a separate screening example if needed and record baseline variation.

Older-adult technology novices can contribute useful general-usability feedback, but their difficulties should not automatically be counted as fine-motor difficulties. Record visual and experience-related barriers so the motor interpretation is clear.

### Sample size and variety

**Working proposal: 3 pilot sessions, then 12 main sessions of about 35 minutes.** This is a manageable formative/exploratory target for the stated deadline, subject to advisor agreement. It is not a statistical power calculation or proof that 12 participants establish efficacy. W3C cautions against generalizing limited user evaluations. [Evaluating with users](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/)

Seek participants from at least two independent community sources, with some adults 50-64 and some 65+, varied experience, and more than one device type if the intervention supports them. Prefer phones for the main sample and treat tablet results separately or exploratorily; pooling different touch geometries can obscure the result. Do not claim representative Gainesville sampling.

## 4. The simplest mobile study

### Resolve the intervention before scheduling

Google states that Chrome Web Store extensions are available on computers and cannot be installed on mobile devices, including desktop-mode mobile browsing. The current repository builds a Chrome MV3 extension. **A regular phone-browser session cannot directly evaluate that desktop extension.** [Google device compatibility](https://support.google.com/chrome_webstore/answer/1698338?hl=en)

The repository's Tune pointing flow already includes touch input and mobile geometry. That establishes implemented mobile calibration support in code, not a verified complete mobile-adaptation study.

For today, choose one research question:

| Study scope | What participants test | Defensible conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mobile calibration/usability** | Existing Tune tasks and fit previews on their phone | Whether calibration and suggested sizes are understandable, usable, and comfortable on the tested devices. |
| **Mobile adaptation comparison** | A controlled web prototype with equivalent baseline and adapted pages | Exploratory changes in tapping performance on those pages. A working prototype must exist before recruitment appointments. |
| **Desktop extension evaluation** | The Chrome extension on a computer | Extension behavior and usability on desktop. This requires changing the mobile-first study scope. |

**Recommended low-effort delivery:** use a mobile website opened by a QR code and a short typed link. Keep the desktop extension as today's separate product demo. If Dr. Rani wants a mobile before/after adaptation claim, use a small controlled prototype and validate its instrumentation first. This report does not create that prototype or certify phone compatibility.

### Proposed 35-minute session

| Minutes | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0-5 | Explain the project, voluntary agreement, setup and accommodations. |
| 5-9 | Brief discussion of actual tapping problems and usual workarounds, then practice. |
| 9-14 | Touch calibration if calibration is part of the research question. |
| 14-27 | Two short, matched task sets on the same device with and without the study adaptation, if implemented. Otherwise observe calibration and preview usability. |
| 27-35 | Ease ratings, comparison interview, breaks as needed, debrief and compensation if offered. |

For a comparison, allocate half the participants baseline-first and half adapted-first. Use matched task versions rather than making everyone repeat an identical answer. Keep the participant's normal settings consistent across conditions, record device/browser/viewport/orientation, and record any changes. Familiarize people with a provided device before measurement. Avoid pooling phone and tablet timings as if their target geometry were equivalent.

Pick **wrong-target activation rate per selection opportunity** as the main motor outcome for an adaptation comparison. Record first-attempt success, task completion, retries, completion time, perceived ease, and any clipping or accidental activation introduced by the adaptation. Separate misses, cancellations, and wrong activations. Report individual paired differences and uncertainty. Avoid describing exploratory numerical differences as established population effects.

Use neutral mock tasks such as selecting an appointment slot or completing a fictional form. Use synthetic content and accounts. Collect only study-page interaction events; whole-phone recordings or personal browsing introduce unnecessary privacy and implementation work. Recording is optional and must follow the project's documented status and participant agreement.

## 5. What “IRB exempt” means for this project

### The low-administration UF route

Your statement that the project is exempt may be correct. UF offers an **automatic nonhuman/exempt/quality-improvement determination tool**. Its HRPP policy says exempt determinations come from the IRB or the UF tool for qualifying categories. A qualifying project can therefore avoid a conventional manually reviewed IRB application while still retaining the official determination. [UF IRB entry point](https://irb.ufl.edu/), [UF HRPP policy, sections 2.1.4-2.1.7](https://research.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/100-050-POLICY-HRP-010-Human-Research-Protection-Program.pdf)

Direct tool: [UF automatic determination](https://research.ufl.edu/services/research-operations-services/secure/hrpp-auto-determination-tool). It requires GatorLink. I verified the official link but could not inspect the authenticated questionnaire; this report does not establish that your exact protocol will qualify. UF lists IRB850 as an option for people using the exempt tool who lack current IRB803 training. [UF training requirements](https://irb.ufl.edu/myirb/training-requirements-for-myirb)

If UF has already issued a determination, retain it and check that it covers the actual mobile activities, data, recruitment, and intended use of the results. If “exempt” currently means an advisor's assessment, use that assessment to complete the UF route or obtain a written answer. No exemption determination was created during this research.

### Three distinct classifications

| Classification | Possible fit to WebAble | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instruction-only class activity outside the regulatory research definition** | May fit an evaluation done solely for classroom instruction/training, with no intended contribution to generalizable knowledge. | UF says plans to publish, present outside the class, or post findings publicly change the analysis. A student project is not automatically outside research rules. [UF classroom guidance, August 2024](https://irb.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/Classroom-Activities-As-Research.pdf) |
| **Exemption 2: surveys/interviews/educational tests/public observation** | May fit an interview/survey component. | Comparing researcher-assigned interface conditions should not automatically be described as public observation or merely a survey. [45 CFR 46.104(d)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.104) |
| **Exemption 3: benign behavioral interventions with adults** | The strongest candidate to examine for brief, voluntary mobile tasks or interface comparisons. | Requires prospective agreement, a brief and benign intervention, permitted data-collection methods, and the applicable identity/disclosure-risk conditions. Some identifiable-data cases require limited IRB review. [45 CFR 46.104(d)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.104) |

OHRP's explanatory material distinguishes brief behavioral interventions from biomedical procedures. Its advisory guidance discusses minor keyboard-like actions incidental to a task. This makes exemption 3 plausible for a mobile usability protocol, but **plausibility is not your institutional determination**. Specify tap coordinates, times, identifiers, recordings, and retention accurately in the UF process; do not assume every form of passive telemetry qualifies. [OHRP revised-rule Q&A](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/sites/default/files/Revised-Common-Rule-Q-and-As-10-25-2018.pdf), [SACHRP advisory interpretation](https://www.hhs.gov/ohrp/sachrp-committee/recommendations/attachment-b-august-2-2017.html)

For the simplest candidate protocol, use adults who can agree independently, short tasks with breaks, no deception, synthetic content, minimal identifiers, and clear opt-in participation. Do not intentionally provoke pain or recruit a medical population as if this were a clinical treatment study. These are study-design recommendations rather than a guarantee of exemption.

**Today’s resolution with Dr. Rani:** identify whether the basis is nonresearch/classroom activity or exempt research, identify the UF documentation already held or needed, and state where the results will go. The generic honors-thesis presentation in repository history does not specify your WebAble study or establish its regulatory status.

Host permission is a separate issue. An exemption does not guarantee that a community organization will distribute the notice or provide a room. UF's recruitment guidance covers study-related communications and materials, so follow the applicable documented route rather than assuming that all notices can be sent immediately. [UF recruitment guidance, March 2026](https://irb.ufl.edu/wp-content/uploads/Guideline-Advertising-and-Recruiting-for-Research-Subjects.pdf)
