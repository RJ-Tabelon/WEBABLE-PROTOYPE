Getting started

What is WebAble?

A Chrome extension that fits websites to how you read, point and focus. It changes things like text size, click targets and motion, shows you each change, and lets you undo it.

What do I need?

Chrome 135 or later on a computer. WebAble works only in Chrome for now. An account is optional, and lets your fit follow you to other computers.

How do I set it up?

Either order works.

  • Add WebAble to Chrome first. It works right away, with small, safe changes on every page. Allow it on sites from its welcome page, then tune it when you like.
  • Or tune it first, then add it to Chrome and connect it to your fit.

Can I tune again, or skip parts?

Yes to both. You can skip any part of Tune, and tune again at any time. Each Tune saves a new version of your fit, so you can go back.

Is there a right answer in Tune?

No. Tune measures what fits you, not how well you do.

What WebAble changes

Changes from this reviewed list, each within a fixed range. On pages for reading, WebAble can also put paragraphs in plainer words. Each one is marked, and a click shows the page’s own words.

Text size
Enlarges running text in the main content to your size; the page scale handles the rest.
Page zoom
Scales this page so its text comes closer to your size. Text, buttons and images grow together.
Line spacing
Adds space between lines and words of running text.
Line length
Keeps long lines of text to a comfortable width.
Contrast
Darkens faint text against its background. Colours and layout stay the same.
Focus outline
Shows a clear outline around whatever the keyboard is on.
Click targets
Makes the clickable area around small links and buttons larger where there’s room, without moving them.
Space between controls
Adds room between links and buttons that sit very close together.
Steady clicks
Counts a click on the button you pressed, even if the pointer slips a little before you let go.
Menus stay open
Keeps menus that open on hover from closing the moment the pointer drifts off.
Moving things
Stops decorative animation and makes transitions instant.
Autoplay
Pauses videos and animated images that start on their own. You can play them again.
Busy areas
Fades ads and side rails so the main content stands out. Hover or tab into them to bring them back.
Focus view
Softens everything except the main content.
Sticky bars
Lets bars that stick to the top of the screen scroll away with the page.
Reading guide
Shades the page above and below the line you are reading.

How do I see what changed?

Select the WebAble button in Chrome’s toolbar. Its “What changed” list shows each change with its before and after values. “Show on page” outlines where each one is.

What doesn’t WebAble change?

It never changes page text, a page’s ARIA, the names of its controls, its focus order or its keyboard handling. It can’t add missing image descriptions or captions, and it can’t change Chrome’s own pages or the Chrome Web Store.

Why did WebAble undo a change on its own?

After every change, WebAble checks the page for problems such as text that overlaps or is cut off. If a change causes one, WebAble puts it back and tells you why in the “What changed” list.

Why did WebAble ask me a question?

When something on a page seems hard to use, WebAble may ask whether to change it, for example “Make buttons bigger on this site?” It never changes anything from what it notices without asking. Choose Not now and it won’t ask again on that site for 7 days.

Undo, pause and turn off

How do I undo a change?

Open WebAble from Chrome’s toolbar and choose Undo next to the change, or Undo all for the whole page. You can also press Alt Shift U on the page.

How do I pause WebAble on one site?

Open WebAble from the toolbar and switch off “WebAble on this site”. The site then looks the way it made itself until you switch it back on.

How do I turn WebAble off everywhere?

Open WebAble, go to Settings, and choose “Turn WebAble off everywhere”. You can also remove the extension from Chrome at any time.

Privacy and site access

What does WebAble send?

Only if you turn on page analysis: the structure of parts of a page, never its text, what you type or which sites you visit. The details are on What WebAble sends.

Why does WebAble ask for access to all sites?

To fit a page as it loads, Chrome requires an extension to have access to that site. WebAble asks once, with a button you choose to press. You can limit it to certain sites in Chrome’s extension settings, and WebAble then works only there.

Keyboard shortcuts

You can change them in Chrome at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.

Move to WebAble’s message on the page
AltShiftW
Undo all changes on this page
AltShiftU
Go back from WebAble’s message
Esc
