How we check our own pages.

WebAble has to work for the people who need it most. Here are the rules we build to, how we check them, and what we know is still missing.

The rules we design to

We use WCAG 2.2 as a checklist and set stricter rules of our own where the research supports it. We don’t claim that this site meets any standard.

Text
Nothing smaller than 15 px, and reading text at 18 px. Headings grow with the page, and everything scales with your browser’s text size.
Contrast
Body text at 7:1 or more and secondary text at 4.5:1 or more, in the light and dark themes. A script computes every colour pair.
Targets
Controls are at least 44 px, and 64 px in Tune, with at least 8 px between them.
Keyboard
Everything works with the keyboard, in reading order, with a visible 3 px focus outline. The first stop on every page skips to the content.
Motion
Nothing moves on its own for more than 5 seconds, nothing loops, and your device’s reduced motion setting turns animation off.
Layout
Pages reflow at 320 px wide, which is 400% zoom on a laptop, with no sideways scrolling.
Your fit
When you’ve tuned WebAble, this site uses your text size, target size, spacing and motion settings too.

How we check

Automated tools find just over half of all problems, so we check by hand as well.

Automated

  • axe-core on every page, in the light and dark themes.
  • A contrast script over every text and background pair in our colour tokens.
  • Unit tests for heading order and for the words we promise never to use.

By hand

  • Keyboard only, from the first tab stop to the footer.
  • With a screen reader, listening for names, roles and announcements.
  • At 200% and 400% zoom, and at 320 px wide.
  • With reduced motion, Windows contrast themes, and text spacing overrides.

The estimate that automated tools find 57% of issues comes from a study by Deque (opens in a new tab).

What we know is missing

  • The sample pages on the home page are pictures made of text. Screen readers hear a description of each one and its changes, not the sample content itself.
  • In those samples, busy areas are faded on purpose, to show how WebAble softens them. Because they are part of a picture, their contrast is lower than the rest of this site.
  • Tune’s pointing task needs a mouse, trackpad or touch screen. If you use a keyboard, switch or voice, you can skip it and set target sizes yourself.
  • Page analysis works only on pages in English.
  • The extension runs only in Chrome 135 or later on a computer.

On other sites

WebAble changes how other sites look and respond for you. It never takes over how they work with assistive technology.

  • It never changes a page’s ARIA, the names of its controls, its focus order or its keyboard handling.
  • Its messages on a page never take focus. Press Alt+Shift+W to move to one, and Esc to go back.
  • It never claims a site is accessible or compliant, because a browser extension can’t make that true.

More on this in the research.
