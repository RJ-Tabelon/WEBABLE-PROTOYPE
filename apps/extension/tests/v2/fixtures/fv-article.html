The last lighthouse keepers of the northern coast

By Mara Linden Article published · October 2, 2026

For more than a century the light at Gull Point was tended by hand. The keepers climbed one hundred and twelve steps every evening, trimmed the wick and wound the clockwork that turned the great lens through the night.

When the station was automated, the last family to live there stayed on as caretakers. They still keep the logbook, a habit that no machine asked them to keep, and they still walk the gallery at dusk.

A life measured in weather

Storms arrive from the west with little warning. The keepers learned to read the colour of the water and the way the gulls flew low over the rocks before the wind turned.

The sea does not care that you are tired. You light the lamp anyway.

What the logbook says

  • Fog on 61 nights in the first year of records.
  • Three shipwrecks avoided, according to the harbour master.
  • One visit from a government minister, who was seasick.
