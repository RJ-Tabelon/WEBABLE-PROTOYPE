River towns plan for a wetter decade

By a staff reporter · Updated just now

Live: 0

Slide one: engineers walked the levee at dawn and counted the places where the water had found a way through last spring.

Slide two: the county will publish its flood maps next month, and residents can comment on them at three public meetings.

Slide three: volunteers planted willows along the bank, which slow the current and hold the soil when the river rises.

The river has crested above the old marker four times in the last ten years, and each time the town has spent the following summer repairing roads, pumping basements and arguing about who should pay for the next round of improvements to the levee system.

This year the council asked engineers for a plan that assumes the wetter seasons will continue. Their draft proposes raising the lowest stretch of road, widening two culverts and buying back a handful of properties that have flooded more than three times.

Residents who spoke at the first meeting were divided. Some want the buyouts to move faster; others worry that the town will lose the families who have lived beside the river for generations and the small businesses that depend on them.

The engineers say the plan can be built in phases, starting with the culverts, which would reduce flooding on the main street even if the rest of the work takes longer to fund than anyone would like.
